## Research Profile

Moonseong (Bronson) Jeong is a PhD candidate in Computer Science at UCLA advised by Prof. Sriram Sankararaman. His work develops scalable statistical and machine learning methods for computational genomics, biomedical imaging, electronic health records, and biobank-scale genetic discovery.

## Research Experience

### Summary-statistics genetic inference and complex-trait architecture

**September 2022–present · Advisor: Sriram Sankararaman**

Develops scalable methods for learning genetic architecture from summary statistics. This work includes SUM-RHE for SNP-heritability estimation and collaborative research on biobank-scale gene–environment interaction, rare-variant gene heritability, and multivariate trait inference.

### Medical-image representations and imaging genetics

**February 2024–present · Advisors: Sriram Sankararaman and Noah Zaitlen**

Develops methods for extracting quantitative, heritable representations from 3D medical images. Current work includes train-free volumetric embeddings and genetic analyses of imaging-derived phenotypes in the UK Biobank.

### Electronic health records and controlled-prescription data

**September 2022–present · Advisor: Sriram Sankararaman**

Develops interpretable machine-learning workflows for governed clinical data, including EHR-based race and ethnicity inference, clinical language modeling, and analysis of California's decade-long CURES controlled-substance prescription data.

### Theoretical limits of local ancestry inference

**July 2022–present · Advisor: April Wei**

Develops a coalescent-theoretic framework for characterizing the limits of reference-matching local ancestry inference under admixture models.

### Biobank-scale heritability-method benchmarking

**May 2020–April 2022 · Advisor: Sriram Sankararaman**

Benchmarked S-LDSC, RHE-mc, and BOLT-REML across simulated genetic architectures and biobank-scale data, including statistical performance analysis and corrections to the RHE-mc implementation.

### MOSDEF Survey

**June 2018–September 2020 · Advisor: Alice Shapley**

Applied Monte Carlo methods and Gaussian-mixture models to high-redshift galaxy spectroscopy, contributing to research on neon emission as a probe of interstellar-medium conditions.
